🌊 Pavement Slab Temperature Curling Simulation

WINKLER SPRING MODEL
Curl Exaggeration:
Engineering Principle: In daytime, solar radiation creates a positive temperature gradient (top hotter than bottom) causing downward convex curl and peak tensile stresses at the slab bottom midway along the longitudinal edge. At nighttime, the slab curls upward (concave), losing contact at corners and joints; wheel loads applied near transverse joints produce critical tensile stresses at the top surface.

⏱️ 24h & 12h-to-12h Traffic (6h Day & 6h Night Peaks)

BUC: 6h DAY • TDC: 6h NIGHT
IRC:58 Fatigue Distribution: 24-hour traffic is split into 12-Hour Day (6 AM – 6 PM) and 12-Hour Night (6 PM – 6 AM). Fatigue is calculated for the critical 6-hour windows:
  • Daytime Peak 6 Hours (10 AM – 4 PM): Bottom-Up Cracking (BUC) under maximum positive temperature curling (+ΔT).
  • Nighttime Peak 6 Hours (0 AM – 6 AM): Top-Down Cracking (TDC) under maximum negative temperature curling (-ΔT).

WHEELBASE vs JOINT SPACING
Top-Down Cracking (TDC) Mechanics: When the distance of vehicle (wheelbase) is less than or equal to the transverse joint spacing (slab length), both front steering and rear drive axles simultaneously load the single slab panel during nighttime curl, generating the peak tensile stress at the top surface.
