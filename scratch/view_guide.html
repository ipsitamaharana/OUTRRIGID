Curl Exaggeration:
IRC:58 Mechanistic Curling Principle
In daytime, solar radiation creates a positive temperature gradient (top hotter than bottom) causing downward convex curl and peak tensile stresses at the slab bottom midway along the longitudinal edge. At nighttime, the slab curls upward (concave), losing contact at corners and joints; wheel loads applied near transverse joints produce critical tensile stresses at the top surface.

IRC:58:2015 provides distinct structural configurations for rigid pavements across Indian highways. Provision of tied concrete shoulders or widened outer lanes reduces edge flexural stresses by up to 30%, resulting in lower slab thickness requirements and substantial concrete material savings.

IRC:58 Clause 5.9 Option 4 & Austroads Composite Pavement Analysis

Bonded Rigid Pavement: Composite PQC + DLC Structural Action

COMPOSITE TWO-LIFT ACTION

When Pavement Quality Concrete (PQC) is laid monolithically over a Dry Lean Concrete (DLC) sub-base without a polythene debonding sheet, both layers act compositely. Due to composite action, the neutral axis shifts downward, providing enhanced combined flexural stiffness ($\text{LHS} \ge \text{RHS}$) that safely permits reducing PQC thickness ($h_1$) from 300 mm to 275 mm.

IRC:58:2015 Theory & Mathematical Formulations Reference Guide

1. Structural Failure Modes & Miner's Cumulative Fatigue Law
Fatigue Criteria
Cumulative Fatigue Damage: Σ CFD = Σ (ni / Ni)BUC + Σ (ni / Ni)TDC ≤ 1.000
Rigid pavement design under IRC:58:2015 evaluates two critical fatigue failure mechanisms: Bottom-Up Cracking (BUC) originating at the longitudinal bottom edge during daytime positive thermal curling, and Top-Down Cracking (TDC) initiating at transverse contraction joint top surfaces under nighttime upward curling. Fatigue damage caused by all commercial axle loads is cumulative over the 30-year design life and must satisfy Palmgren-Miner's hypothesis (≤ 1.000).
2. Climatic Zones & Temperature Differentials (ΔT)
IRC:58 Table 1 • Temperature Gradients
Daytime Gradient (+ΔT) = Top Hotter → Downward Curling (BUC)
Nighttime Gradient (-ΔT) = Top Cooler → Upward Curling (TDC)
Effective Night ΔT = -13.4°C + (-5.0°C Permanent Built-in Curl) = -18.4°C (Zone III)
India is partitioned into 7 distinct climatic zones. For Zone III (Gangetic Plains / Eastern Delta) as modeled in standard design benchmarks, daytime maximum temperature curling is +16.8°C and nighttime warping is -13.4°C for a ~300mm slab.
3. Design Commercial Traffic Volume & Axle Repetitions
Traffic Mechanics • Clause 4
N = [365 · A · ((1 + r)n - 1) / r] · D · F
Expected Repetitions: ni = N · (Frequency % / 100) · Split Factor
Where A = initial 2-way traffic (6,000 CVPD), r = annual growth rate (5.0%), n = design life (30 years), D = directional distribution (50%), and F = design lane factor (25% for 4-lane divided highway per Table 4.1).
4. Modulus of Subgrade Reaction (k) & Composite Subbase Foundations
IRC:58 Tables 2 & 3 • Subbase Foundation
Subgrade CBR 8% → Subgrade k = 50.33 MPa/m (0.0503 N/mm³)
Over 150mm GSB: Composite keff = 58.14 MPa/m (0.05814 N/mm³) → Granular Base
Over 150mm DLC: Composite keff = 284.67 MPa/m (0.28467 N/mm³) → Lean Concrete Base
Provision of a 150mm Dry Lean Concrete (DLC) subbase drastically increases foundation stiffness from 58.14 MPa/m to 284.67 MPa/m, dampening flexural slab deflections and enabling a reduction in required concrete slab depth.
5. Westergaard Radius of Relative Stiffness (l) & Edge Stresses
Westergaard • IITSLAB-II
l = [ E · h3 / (12 · k · (1 - μ2)) ]1/4
σTDC = -0.219 + [1.686 · β · Peff · h / (k · l4)] + [168.48 · h2 / (k · l2)] + 0.1089 · ΔTnight
Radius l reflects slab flexural stiffness versus foundation resilience. For Trial I (h=310mm, GSB), l = 1.084 m; for Trial II (h=275mm, GSB), l = 0.991 m; for DLC Base (h=280mm), l = 0.675 m.
6. Concrete Fatigue Criteria (PCA / IRC:58 Model)
Fatigue Life Formulation
Stress Ratio (SR) = σcombined / fcr
If SR < 0.45: Ni = ∞ (Infinite Life, Fatigue Damage = 0)
If 0.45 ≤ SR ≤ 0.55: Ni = [ 4.2577 / (SR - 0.4325) ]3.268
If SR > 0.55: log10(Ni) = (0.9718 - SR) / 0.0828
When wheel load stress ratio is less than 0.45, concrete behaves elastically with zero microcrack accumulation. For higher stress ratios, allowable repetitions Ni decrease exponentially.
7. Bonded Concrete Pavement (Composite PQC + DLC Mechanics)
Composite PQC + DLC Mechanics
Neutral Axis Depth: d = [ 0.5 · E1 · h12 + E2 · h2 · (h1 + 0.5 · h2) ] / [ E1 · h1 + E2 · h2 ]
D1 = [ E1 / (1 - μ12) ] · [ h13/12 + h1 · (d - 0.5 · h1)2 ]
D2 = [ E2 / (1 - μ22) ] · [ h23/12 + h2 · (h1 + 0.5 · h2 - d)2 ]
Condition for Safety: LHS (D1 + D2) ≥ RHS [ E1 · htarget3 / (12(1 - μ12)) ]
By eliminating the separation membrane and creating a monolithic shear bond between 275mm PQC (M40) and 150mm DLC (M10), the transformed composite section yields LHS = 69,976.3 MN·mm, which exceeds the required 300mm unbonded slab rigidity RHS = 69,053.7 MN·mm (Safety Margin = +922.6 MN·mm).
8. Contraction Joint Dowel Bars & Longitudinal Tie Bars
Joint & Tie Bar Mechanics
Dowel Bearing Stress: σb = K · y0 ≤ 0.83 · fck · (4 - d) / 3
Tie Bar Tensile Steel Area: As = (γc · h · W · f) / fs
Smooth dowel bars transfer 50% wheel shear across transverse joints without resisting thermal expansion/contraction. Deformed tie bars across longitudinal joints resist lateral lane separation caused by subgrade friction.
